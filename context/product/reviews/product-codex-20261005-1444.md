### F1: WhatsApp fallback lacks the required justification
- severity: major
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:Messenger decision
- problem: The fallback is conditional on inability to implement MAX; the definition records a preference without establishing that condition.
- evidence: The brief says “if you cannot do it for MAX”; the definition only says WhatsApp was chosen.
- fix: State the concrete MAX constraint that necessitates the fallback, or retain MAX.

### F2: Messenger fallback does not authorize changing the visual prototype
- severity: major
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§3.1
- problem: The WhatsApp Web substitution is flagged as an assumption but is unsupported by the brief.
- evidence: Requirement 4 explicitly names web.max.ru; the fallback clause concerns the messenger.
- fix: Keep MAX’s visual prototype while using WhatsApp transport, unless a prototype change is explicitly accepted.

### F3: Credentials alone do not establish receiving readiness
- severity: major
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§2.1 Credentials login
- problem: An authorized instance can pass login yet never supply replies through polling because required notification settings are absent.
- evidence: Public [GREEN-API HTTP receiving documentation](https://green-api.com/en/docs/api/receiving/technology-http-api/) requires an empty `webhookUrl` and enabled incoming notifications; the definition checks only existence and authorization.
- fix: Add documented instance prerequisites and a readiness check with console setup guidance; require no competing notification consumer during review.

### F4: “Once handled” leaves queue-blocking and duplicate behavior undefined
- severity: major
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§2.1 Receive text messages
- problem: Unsupported events, failed acknowledgement, and repeated or late messages have no defined outcome. Ignoring an event without acknowledging it can prevent subsequent replies from appearing.
- evidence: The public [HTTP receiving documentation](https://green-api.com/en/docs/api/receiving/technology-http-api/) describes a FIFO queue with explicit deletion; “once handled” does not define handling for excluded media, groups, or statuses.
- fix: Require acknowledgement of deliberately skipped events, durable storage before acknowledging supported messages, duplicate suppression, and a consistent message ordering rule.

### F5: Immediate outgoing bubbles can falsely imply successful sending
- severity: major
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§2.1 Send text messages
- problem: Pending, rejected, and uncertain sends are indistinguishable. A timeout followed by retry may send the same message twice.
- evidence: “Sent messages shown right away” precedes confirmation; public [SendMessage documentation](https://green-api.com/en/docs/api/sending/SendMessage/) describes queue acceptance, not recipient delivery.
- fix: Distinguish pending, API-accepted, failed, and unknown outcomes; retain failed text and prohibit automatic retry of uncertain sends.

### F6: Offline and authorization-loss recovery are unspecified
- severity: major
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§1.4 Errors; §2.1 Credentials login
- problem: A generic error message does not define what happens when connectivity or authorization disappears after login, including after restoring saved credentials.
- evidence: Authorization is checked “before entering the chat”; no subsequent pause, recovery, or revalidation behavior is stated.
- fix: Define a disconnected state, preserve chats and drafts, pause unavailable operations, resume polling after recovery, and direct unauthorized users to reauthorize in the console.

### F7: Multiple tabs undermine persistence and logout promises
- severity: major
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§2.2; §3.1 localStorage
- problem: Two tabs can consume the same instance queue and overwrite shared chat storage. Logout in one tab can leave another using cached credentials or restoring cleared data.
- evidence: Persistent credentials and chats are required, but poller ownership and cross-tab logout are unspecified.
- fix: Limit the app to one active tab per instance, visibly block additional tabs, and propagate logout to every tab.

### F8: Phone input and incoming-chat routing lack an observable contract
- severity: minor
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§2.1 New chat; Receive text messages
- problem: Accepted phone formats and normalization are unspecified, as is the treatment of incoming text without an existing matching chat.
- evidence: “Enter the recipient’s phone number” and “routed into the matching chat” permit implementations that create duplicate conversations or silently drop unfamiliar senders.
- fix: Define the required international format, normalize equivalent inputs to one chat, and explicitly choose whether unmatched incoming text creates a chat or is discarded.

### F9: Success metrics are subjective and contradict the scope
- severity: minor
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§1.4; §3.1
- problem: “A few seconds,” “recognizably,” and “no features beyond the brief” lack consistent pass criteria. Required persistence and optional history already exceed the brief’s explicit feature set.
- evidence: §3.1 adds reload persistence and `GetChatHistory`, while §1.4 prohibits additional features.
- fix: Specify a measured latency threshold and test conditions, use a visual checklist against the required prototype, and replace the scope claim with an explicit minimal feature list; defer history.

### F10: Submission checklist omits mandatory items and upgrades optional ones
- severity: major
- where: context/product/product-definition.md:§1.4 Final success metric
- problem: The claimed complete submission omits the resume, Telegram handle, work preference, email destination, and subject. It also presents deployment and screenshots/video as brief-mandated.
- evidence: The brief requires the omitted items, calls deployment “desirable,” and requests screenshots/video only “if any.”
- fix: Mirror the brief’s mandatory submission checklist and label deployment/demo assets as optional or self-imposed commitments.

## Verdict
DO NOT SHIP

The definition leaves source-brief deviations unresolved and omits receiving prerequisites and failure behavior needed for a reliable end-to-end review.
