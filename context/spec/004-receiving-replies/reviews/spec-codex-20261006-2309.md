### F1: Persistence failure can lead to acknowledging an unsaved reply
- severity: major
- where: technical-considerations.md:§2.4–§2.5
- problem: A synchronous store update does not guarantee localStorage succeeded. No recovery behavior is defined for quota or storage-write failures.
- evidence: The loop assumes “localStorage is written before the call returns.” If memory updates but persistence throws, redelivery can hit dedupe and proceed to deletion without saving the reply durably.
- fix: Require confirmed persistence before acknowledgement, including on duplicate deliveries; retain the notification and surface a storage error when persistence fails.

### F2: The reconnect deadline exceeds the loop's timeout guarantees
- severity: major
- where: functional-spec.md:§2.4; technical-considerations.md:§2.4
- problem: The proposed loop cannot guarantee replies within 10 seconds after connectivity returns.
- evidence: A stalled receive can consume its remaining 30-second budget, or a delete its 15-second budget, before retrying. Capping backoff at five seconds does not bound those waits.
- fix: Either define recovery that interrupts stalled requests on reconnect, with a test covering that case, or relax the deadline to include outstanding request budgets.

### F3: Failed-delete handling contradicts the retry policy
- severity: major
- where: technical-considerations.md:§2.4
- problem: “Any other HTTP answer” treats authentication failures and arbitrary server failures as permission to move on, although the notification may remain queued. This also contradicts the stated backoff for all 4xx and 5xx responses.
- evidence: A successful receive resets backoff; a repeated DELETE 500 then immediately returns to receive, repeatedly fetching the same head without progressing.
- fix: Treat only verified already-deleted outcomes as success. Retry other delete failures with backoff that successful re-receives do not reset.

### F4: Malformed envelopes can block receiving forever
- severity: major
- where: technical-considerations.md:§2.2, §2.4, §3 risk 5
- problem: The claim that malformed notifications cannot block the queue excludes envelopes rejected before mapping. Those retry forever without acknowledgement or a visible failure.
- evidence: A missing numeric `receiptId` throws `badBody`; `badBody` retries indefinitely against a FIFO head that remains until deleted.
- fix: Separate malformed bodies with usable receipt IDs from unacknowledgeable envelopes. Define a visible blocked-queue state for the latter and qualify the unconditional non-blocking promise.

### F5: Late replies from new senders have contradictory ordering
- severity: minor
- where: functional-spec.md:§2.2
- problem: QA cannot determine where a newly discovered chat containing an old reply belongs.
- evidence: List order “always” follows latest-message time, but a number with no existing chat must appear “at the top.”
- fix: State that new chats also follow latest-message time; limit the top-of-list criterion to replies newer than existing activity.

## Verdict
DO NOT SHIP

The draft leaves durable acknowledgement and queue recovery unsafe or undefined, and promises a reconnect deadline its proposed loop cannot guarantee.
